### Flow Systems
1. Conventional flow system (much more common)
	- electrons move from positive to negative 
	- positive means that there are alot of electrons there (high voltage to low voltage)
2. Electron flow system
	- electrons move from negative to positive
	- positive/negative means the charge of that area (negative means that there many electrons there)
### Polarization vs Non-polarization
1. Polarized
	- one direction only
	- this means that electricity enters from one end and exits the other
	- ex) diodes, symbolized by arrow and flat line. flat line represents infinite [[Electronics 101#^be79bd|resistance]] and arrow represents direction of flow. one way gate
2. Non-polarized
	- no direction
	- ex) Lamps
### Electricity
1. Static electricity
	- rubbing one object on another makes the electrons move from one to another
	- called "static" because the electrons only move from one surface to another 
2. Coulomb
	- one coulomb of charge is = to 6,250,000,000,000,000,000 electrons (6.25 quintillion)
3. [[Electronics 101#^08b8c1|Ohm's Law]] (sometimes you will have to solve problems with this) ^9c02c6
	- E=IR
	- R=E/I
	- I=E/R
4. Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL)
	- Total [[Electronics 101#^936185|current]] in = total current out (this means that the energy cannot be lost, what goes in must come out)
5. Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL)
	- sum of all differences in [[Electronics 101#^1f22f6|voltage]] = 0 in a close circuit (this means that energy cannot be lost, meaning that whatever negative voltage is made up by the positive voltage)

3. Electrolytic / Tantalum Capacitors: Curved bar + straight bar
4. [[Electronics 101#^21303d|Diodes]]/LEDs: Arrow into straight bar
